# Known failure modes of AI-assisted research

Each skill in this library targets one failure mode in the table below. All evidence in the table comes from things that actually happened in the author's own projects; speculation is not included. When you encounter a new problem, first record its symptoms and evidence here; consider turning it into a skill only after the same kind of problem has occurred twice or more.

The evidence comes from the author's projects in quantum information, mathematical modeling, computational mathematics, software measurement and quantitative strategy research. These failure modes are not limited to those fields, and the skills are implemented to work across disciplines. For example:
- Items 1 and 2 show up in clinical and social science as writing associations as causation, or as over-hedging exploratory results and shying away from any conclusion;
- Item 3 shows up in empirical research as test statistics that are inconsistent with their p-values.

Cases from other fields are welcome in the "Observed evidence" column.

| # | Failure mode | Symptoms | Observed evidence | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Defensive writing buries the novelty** | Hedges stacked layer upon layer; "we do not claim" as a standalone sentence; results buried further back; contributions listed with equal weight; the gap is stated without naming prior work | A manuscript was rejected twice by editors for "lack of clarity"; the abstract was 497 words, with the main result in sentence 7 (`skills/paper-polish/references/case-studies.md`, Case A) | ✅ `paper-polish` |
| 2 | **Overshooting in the opposite direction** | When asked to "write more forcefully", stating conditional results as unconditional; overusing law, first, optimal; computing improvement factors against straw-man baselines | One mock review pointed out that a conclusion depending on an unproven assumption read in the abstract as if it were established; the title used a stronger noun than what the proof delivers; the abstract opened with an asymptotic result that is vacuous at practical parameters (Case B) | ✅ the claim ledger in `paper-polish`; could become a standalone `claim-audit` |
| 3 | **Drift in numbers and conventions** | Copying a tool's default conventions without reading its source code; the same symbol taking two sets of values in different places; parameters already ruled out by a counterexample still plotted in the main figure | An external tool's precision parameter was interpreted under the wrong distance definition, shifting all "measured" numbers systematically; the same quantity differed by a factor of about 4 between a worked example in the introduction and the main theorem; a constant already ruled out by a counterexample in the text was still used in the main figure | ✅ `number-audit` (audit, then guards that recompute key numbers on every build) |
| 4 | **Erroneous or hallucinated bibliographic metadata** | Wrong volume numbers, authors, affiliations, or years, or even fabricated references | 8 errors were found in the user's literature survey file; for one paper, the affiliation and hardware platform were entirely wrong | ✅ `cite-verify` (the exemplar-paper verification in paper-polish uses the same interface) |
| 5 | **Self-confirming novelty** | When searching, AI tends to find literature supporting "we are new" and misses work that reviewers will use for comparison | The main theorem was judged to be a standard multi-pass streaming argument; the original manuscript did not cite key references such as Guha–McGregor (Case A). In a computational project, a planned direction was pre-empted by a preprint, and one day after an independent reproduction was started, another group deposited one on Zenodo | ✅ `novelty-audit` (audit mode; scout mode with a watch list before and during an investment) |
| 6 | **Sycophantic progress reporting** | When the user repeatedly asks to crack an open problem, AI presents partial progress as a breakthrough, or repeatedly retries routes that have already failed | In a long-running mathematical effort, when repeatedly asked to "push it to the optimum", it was necessary to state honestly that all routes reduce to the same unsolved proposition; the failed routes needed an index, marked "do not redo" | ✅ `dead-end-ledger` |
| 7 | **Mock review lacking independence** | Reviewer subagents are led astray after reading the author's notes; errors claimed by subagents are accepted without verification | One mock review deliberately did not read the author's joint report; the main session recomputed each of the errors it raised one by one, most held up, and one needed a correction in its details | ✅ `mock-review` (Phase 7 of paper-polish is a lightweight version) |
| 8 | **Choosing where to submit based on impressions** | A journal's positioning, bar, fees, and deadlines are recalled from memory, leading to misjudgments | Only after checking official pages during one survey was it found that ACM TQC is a journal rather than a conference, that STOC 2027 has a new LLM disclosure policy, that a talk submission is not automatically considered for the poster track, and so on | ✅ `venue-fit` |
| 9 | **The code does not do what the paper says; the design manufactures the result** | A claimed design (pairing, seeds, splits, sample size) is not what the code implements; random draws inside conditions desynchronize the scenarios; sentinel and fallback values reach result tables; small samples hide failures; instrumentation or round order creates effects; the project's own checks pass falsely | After all automated checks of a modeling paper passed, two adversarial rounds found 29 problems, including a claimed paired comparison run on different seeds, sentinel values reported as results, and a livelock hidden by a sample of eight. In a software measurement study, an observer effect, server-side state carried across rounds, and an undelimited filename match each produced a false "major finding". A backtest's best configuration out of 32 was not significant out of sample | ✅ `experiment-integrity` |
| 10 | **Proof steps that nobody checked** | Long chains of estimates that look complete; "clearly", "similarly" and "standard argument" over nontrivial steps; unspecified constants; a result that silently depends on a conjecture; a solver's verdict taken as proof of the formula fed to it | In a theory manuscript, the headline theorem rested on an assembly step that was never written as a human-checkable proof; two solvers checked the same formula, which validates the arithmetic but not its derivation from the lemmas; a reduction the solver check depended on was asserted without proof; several lemmas carried sketched steps and unspecified constants; independent reviews of other parts returned VALID only after fixes, and an interpretation of numerical evidence was withdrawn | ✅ `proof-audit` |

Candidates observed but not yet written as skills (each has occurred in the author's projects):
- **compute-budget**: paid or long computations launched without a cost estimate from an unbiased pilot (one batch cost about three times the estimate and overdrew the account; work was lost without checkpoints), and long jobs watched by sleep-polling instead of being handed over with an ETA.
- **submission-check**: format red lines and required statements of a venue (page limits, headers, AI-use statements), identity leaks in supplementary material (team numbers, home-directory paths), cover letters left addressed to the previous journal, builds that break on a new TeX distribution.
- **release-guard**: private material pushed to public repositories or the wrong branch (a server address in public history, a research branch repeatedly copied over a submission branch).
- **talk-deck**: research slides rewritten without consent, hedged, or given numbers that replaced the author's stated figures; talk length estimated instead of counted.
- **reviewer-response**: not yet observed; to be written after the first real referee report.

## When to write a new skill

- The same failure mode has occurred at least twice, or it occurred once and caused real damage (e.g., a rejection, or wrong numbers in print).
- The countermeasure can be written as an executable procedure, ideally accompanied by a script that checks the results, rather than just a reminder like "watch out for X".
- Write it following `templates/SKILL_TEMPLATE.md`, and register it in the table above when done.
